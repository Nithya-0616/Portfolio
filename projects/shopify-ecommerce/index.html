Shopify E-Commerce Performance

An executive Power BI dashboard for three years of online sales, built to find where revenue comes from and where it leaks back out as returns.

The question

An online store selling across seven countries needed one place to see how the business was really doing: which categories drive revenue, which markets matter most, and how much of that revenue is lost to returns.

The data

Shopify sales data covering 2023 to 2025, with orders, quantities, revenue, product category, country, payment method, customer rating and a returned-order flag for every line.

What I did

  • Cleaned and shaped the raw export in Power Query, fixing data types and preparing it for modelling.
  • Wrote DAX measures for total revenue, distinct orders, quantity sold, average order value, returned orders, return rate and a dynamic “top category” card.
  • Designed a one-page executive view: KPI cards on top, then monthly revenue and orders, top categories, return rate by category, payment methods, rating bands and top countries.
  • Added slicers for year, product category and country so a manager can answer their own follow-up questions.

Two of the DAX measures behind the KPI cards:

Return Rate % = DIVIDE( [Returned Orders], [Total Orders] ) Average Order Value = DIVIDE([Total Revenue], [Total Orders])

What the data showed

  • Electronics is the top category by revenue, though the top five categories sit close together at about 20% each.
  • Roughly 1 in 7 orders comes back. The overall return rate is 14.8%, and Sports is highest at 15.2%, followed by Fashion and Beauty at 15.0%.
  • Australia is the biggest market at about 8.7M, but Germany, the UAE, India and Canada are all within a few hundred thousand.
  • Revenue drops in the second half of the year. Monthly revenue sits around 5.5–6.3M from January to May, then falls to around 4M from July onwards.

What I'd recommend

  • Investigate why Sports and Fashion are returned more often (sizing, product descriptions, quality) before scaling ad spend there.
  • Study what makes Electronics perform, and apply it to the categories just behind it.
  • Plan stock and promotions around the mid-year revenue drop instead of reacting to it.
  • Use payment method and rating trends to smooth checkout and catch product issues early.
