Pizza Sales Analysis

A full year of pizza orders turned into a Power BI dashboard on best sellers, busy hours and the ingredients that matter most for stock planning.

The question

A pizza restaurant wanted to know which pizzas earn the most, when customers order, how sizes and categories contribute, and which ingredients it uses most, so it can staff and stock more accurately.

The data

48,620 order lines across 12 columns for January to December 2015: order and pizza IDs, date and time, quantity, unit and total price, size, category and a combined ingredients field.

What I did

  • Prepared the data in Power Query and kept the original transaction table intact for accurate order-level maths.
  • Solved a tricky field: each pizza listed all its ingredients in one cell. Split it into one row per ingredient, cleaned and grouped it, and aggregated quantities into a separate ingredient-level table.
  • Wrote DAX measures for revenue, distinct orders, pizzas sold and average pizzas per order.
  • Built visuals for top five pizzas, monthly trend, peak ordering hours, size contribution, category sales and the five most-used ingredients.

What the data showed

  • 21,350 orders and 49,574 pizzas brought in about $817.9K over the year.
  • Customers buy 2.32 pizzas per order on average, at an average order value of $38.31, which suggests most orders are for groups or families.
  • The ingredient-level table shows which toppings drive stock use, something the original data could not answer directly.
  • Ordering hours and monthly trends show clear peaks, useful for planning kitchen staff and prep.

What I'd recommend

  • Schedule more kitchen staff and pre-prep for peak ordering hours.
  • Prioritise stock of the most-used ingredients to avoid running out of best sellers.
  • Build combo deals around multi-pizza orders, since most customers already buy more than two.
